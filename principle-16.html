Timeless Principle No. 16

Security and Welfare Must Go Together.

Author, Veteran Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur

"The purpose of governance is Yogakshema—prosperity protected by security."

Every citizen expects two things from the State: the opportunity to prosper and the security to enjoy what has been earned. One without the other is incomplete. Kautilya expressed this ideal through the concept of Yogakshema (योगक्षेम)—the acquisition of prosperity and its secure enjoyment.

For Kautilya, this was the central responsibility of government. The ruler was expected to defend the realm, maintain internal order, administer justice, support agriculture, encourage trade and ensure that economic activity could flourish. Security and prosperity were never separate objectives; each depended upon the other.

Prosperity has little meaning unless people can enjoy it in peace. Kautilya therefore saw security not as an end in itself, but as the condition that enables citizens to preserve and enjoy the fruits of their labour.

This principle remains equally relevant today. Nations attract investment where both law and order and economic opportunity exist together. Businesses grow where people feel secure. Families flourish where they enjoy both safety and opportunity. The same is true in personal life, where lasting success depends upon building prosperity while safeguarding what has been achieved.

Kautilya also understood that threats to Yogakshema arise from many sources—foreign aggression, crime, corrupt officials, deceitful merchants, administrative negligence and natural calamities. Good governance therefore demands constant vigilance against every force that undermines either security or welfare.

Kautilya reminds us that the highest purpose of governance is Yogakshema—a society in which people can create prosperity, preserve it securely and enjoy it in peace.

Security protects prosperity. Prosperity strengthens security. Together they constitute the true purpose of governance.
