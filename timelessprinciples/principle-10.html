Timeless Principle No. 10

Power Must Always Serve Purpose.

· Author, Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur (Retd)

"Power is a means to secure the welfare of the state, never an end in itself".

Power has always fascinated people. It commands attention, inspires ambition and often becomes the measure of success. Kautilya saw it differently. He regarded power not as an achievement to be admired, but as a responsibility to be exercised wisely. Power was never meant for personal glory. It existed to protect the state, preserve order and promote the prosperity of the people. This distinction shaped his entire philosophy of leadership.

A ruler who pursued power for its own sake eventually weakened the very kingdom he sought to control. Personal ambition, unchecked authority and the misuse of office diverted attention from the true purpose of governance.

Power, in Kautilya's hands, was always linked to duty. Its legitimacy depended upon the results it produced.

The same principle applies today. Leadership positions, public office and organisational authority exist to advance a larger purpose. When leaders begin to serve their position instead of the mission it represents, institutions gradually lose direction and public confidence begins to erode.

Authority carries an obligation. Every important decision should answer a simple question: Whom does this serve? If the answer is only the leader, power has already lost its purpose. If the answer is the institution and the people it exists to serve, power becomes a force for stability and progress.

Kautilya reminds us that enduring leadership is never measured by the amount of power one holds. It is measured by the purpose that power fulfils.

"Power is justified only when it serves a purpose greater than itself.
