Timeless Principle No. 14

Justice Is the Foundation of Public Trust.

Author, Veteran Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur

"Justice earns the trust that sustains the State."

Every society expects justice because justice creates confidence. When laws are applied impartially, people cooperate willingly, invest with confidence and respect public institutions. When justice becomes uncertain or selective, trust begins to disappear long before disorder becomes visible.

Kautilya regarded justice as one of the indispensable pillars of good governance. A ruler's authority could not rest on power alone. It had to be sustained by a legal system that protected the innocent, resolved disputes fairly and ensured that punishment was proportionate to the offence. Justice was not merely an instrument of control; it was the foundation of public confidence.

This principle remains equally relevant today. Governments derive legitimacy from the fairness of their institutions. Businesses prosper when contracts are enforced impartially. Universities, professional bodies and organisations earn respect when rules apply equally to everyone. The same is true in personal life, where fairness and integrity build trust that no position or authority can command.

The opposite is equally true. When justice is delayed, inconsistent or influenced by power and privilege, confidence steadily erodes. People begin to rely on influence rather than institutions, and society gradually drifts towards what Kautilya called Matsyanyaya (मत्स्यन्याय)—the "law of the fish," where the strong prey upon the weak in the absence of effective justice.

Kautilya reminds us that justice does far more than punish wrongdoing. It strengthens public order, supports prosperity and prevents society from descending into Matsyanyaya.

Justice does more than punish wrongdoing—it creates the trust upon which enduring governance is built.
