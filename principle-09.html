Timeless Principle No. 09

A leader must first govern himself.

Author, Veteran Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur

The first victory of leadership is mastery over oneself.

Leadership is often measured by the ability to influence others. Acharya Kautilya measured it differently. He believed that before a ruler could govern a kingdom, command an army or administer justice, he must first learn to govern himself.

For Acharya Kautilya, leadership was as much a matter of character as of competence. He warned rulers against becoming captive to anger, greed, pride, indulgence or impatience. These were not merely personal failings. Left unchecked, they distorted judgment, encouraged poor decisions and gradually weakened the institutions the ruler was entrusted to protect.

Self-discipline therefore became the foundation of wise governance. A leader who exercises restraint inspires confidence. One who controls his emotions makes better decisions. One who places duty above personal interest earns lasting respect.

This principle extends far beyond government. In every organisation, people observe the conduct of those who lead them. Standards are shaped not only by written policies but by everyday behaviour. Integrity, humility and consistency at the top gradually become part of the culture throughout the institution. Leadership is contagious — and so is indiscipline.

The greatest challenge of leadership is rarely the management of others. It is the management of oneself. A leader who masters himself has already overcome the most difficult obstacle to wise governance.

The first responsibility of leadership is self-mastery.
