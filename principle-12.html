Timeless Principle No. 12

The Welfare of the People Is the Purpose of the State.

Author, Veteran Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur

"The ruler exists for the welfare of the people."

Power is often mistaken for the purpose of government. Kautilya saw it differently. He believed that the state does not exist to serve the ruler. The ruler exists to serve the state, and the state exists to secure the welfare of its people.

Every institution of governance-the treasury, the administration, the courts, the armed forces and diplomacy-has value only when it contributes to the security, prosperity and well-being of the people. Power is never an end in itself. It is a public trust. Kautilya recognised that a ruler who pursues personal ambition at the expense of the people ultimately weakens the kingdom. By contrast, a ruler who protects their welfare strengthens the very foundations of the state.

This principle extends far beyond government. The finest organisations exist to serve their customers rather than their executives. The strongest institutions remember why they were created and whom they are meant to benefit. Leadership earns lasting respect when people believe that their interests not personal prestige-guide every important decision. Public welfare is therefore not merely an act of compassion. It is the highest expression of responsible leadership.

Kautilya understood that when leaders place people at the centre of governance, trust deepens, institutions become stronger and the state acquires enduring legitimacy. That is why the Arthashastra remains far more than a manual of power. It is a philosophy of responsible governance.

The purpose of the state is fulfilled only when the welfare of the people is protected.
