Timeless Principle No. 08

Justice Creates Loyalty.

· Author, Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur (Retd)

"People obey authority. They remain loyal to justice."

Power can command obedience. Fear can enforce discipline. But neither can create lasting loyalty. Kautilya understood that the strength of a kingdom depended not only upon its army or its treasury, but upon the confidence of its people in the fairness of its administration. A ruler who governed with justice earned something that force alone could never secure-the willing support of the people.

For Kautilya, justice was not an act of generosity. It was a duty of governance. Laws had to be applied fairly. Disputes had to be resolved impartially. Officials had to be held accountable. When justice became predictable, trust followed. When justice became arbitrary, confidence in the state began to erode.

This principle remains as relevant today as it was over two thousand years ago. Organisations flourish when employees believe they are treated fairly. Institutions earn respect when rules are applied consistently. Families remain united when decisions are seen as impartial rather than preferential. Justice does more than settle disputes. It strengthens relationships.

Kautilya recognised that people willingly support institutions they trust. That trust cannot be purchased, nor can it be imposed. It is earned through fairness, consistency and integrity.

A leader may possess authority by virtue of office. But legitimacy is earned through justice. That is why Kautilya regarded justice not merely as a legal principle, but as one of the strongest foundations of stable governance.

Justice does not merely preserve order. It creates loyalty."
