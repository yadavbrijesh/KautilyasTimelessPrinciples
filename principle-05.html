Timeless Principle No. 05

Intelligence is the first line of defence.

Author, Veteran Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur

A ruler who sees clearly rarely needs to act in haste.

Leadership is often associated with decisive action. Acharya Kautilya understood that decisive action begins with accurate information. A ruler who remains unaware of the condition of his own kingdom, the intentions of rivals, or the sentiments of the people, is governing in darkness.

For this reason, Acharya Kautilya regarded intelligence as an essential function of good governance. Information had to be observed, verified and corroborated before it influenced policy. Information is valuable only when it is reliable.

Rumour excites. Intelligence enlightens.

This principle extends far beyond government. Successful organisations understand markets before making investments. Effective leaders listen before deciding. Strong institutions encourage the free flow of accurate information, because problems concealed today become crises tomorrow.

Acharya Kautilya recognised that leaders who lose touch with facts gradually lose the ability to govern effectively. Ignorance is rarely harmless — it simply delays reality.

His lesson is therefore timeless. Leadership is not merely the ability to make decisions. It is the ability to make informed decisions. Knowledge does not eliminate uncertainty, but it greatly reduces avoidable mistakes.

The quality of leadership depends upon the quality of information available to it.
