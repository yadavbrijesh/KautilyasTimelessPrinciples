Timeless Principle No. 02

Why readiness matters more than action.

· Veteran Wg Cdr Vijay Dhanraj Chattur

The best decisions are made long before they are announced.

Decisive leadership is often admired because we see only the moment of decision. We rarely see the long period of preparation that made that decision possible.

More than two thousand years ago, Kautilya recognised this truth with remarkable clarity. Before considering what action should be taken, he insisted on answering a more fundamental question: is the kingdom ready? For Kautilya, readiness was never assumed — it had to be examined.

Before undertaking any major enterprise, the ruler was expected to assess the condition of the state itself. Was the administration efficient? Could the treasury sustain the effort? Was the army disciplined and prepared? Did the people remain prosperous and supportive? Were allies dependable? Had internal disturbances been resolved? Only after this assessment did Kautilya consider the appropriate course of action.

His insight remains profoundly relevant today. Modern leadership often celebrates speed. Yet enduring success rarely comes from those who simply act quickly — it comes from those who have prepared thoroughly long before the moment of decision arrives. What appears to be decisive action is often the visible expression of careful preparation, disciplined thinking and quiet readiness.

Readiness, therefore, is neither confidence nor optimism. It is an honest assessment of one's capabilities and limitations. Leaders who neglect this assessment often substitute hope for preparation and urgency for strategy. Kautilya warned against such thinking. He understood that success does not belong to those who act first, but to those who act when they are ready.

Whether governing a nation, leading an organisation or making an important personal decision, Kautilya's sequence never changed: first examine your readiness, then commit yourself to action.

Before committing to action, first ensure that you are prepared to sustain it.
